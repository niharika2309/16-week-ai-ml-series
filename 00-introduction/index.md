# 16 Weeks to AI: Let’s Stop Feeling Like We’re Behind

*August 25, 2026 · [Originally published on Substack](https://niharikabatra.substack.com/p/16-weeks-to-ai-lets-stop-feeling)*

*An open learning journey for women transitioning into AI - one concept at a time.*

It’s almost the end of the year.

September. October. November. December.

**Just 16 weeks.**

You may have started the year thinking:

*“This is the year I’m finally going to get serious about AI.”*

Then AI moved faster than you could keep up with.

New models.  
New frameworks.  
Agents.  
RAG.  
MCP.  
Multimodal AI.  
Vector databases.  
AI engineering.  
LLMs.

And suddenly, instead of feeling excited about AI, you start feeling like you’re already behind.

Especially when you’re trying to **transition into AI from another technical role.**

You open a job description and see 25 different technologies.

You read an AI paper and understand 60% of it.

You watch someone build an AI agent in 15 minutes and think:

*“How does everyone know all of this?”*

I’ve felt that too.

And I’ve realized something.

**We don’t necessarily need more AI content.**

We need **better paths through the content.**

**So I’m starting something.**

For the next **16 weeks**, I’m going to break down AI **one concept at a time**.

Not as a “learn AI in 16 weeks” challenge.

And definitely not as a collection of random tutorials.

Instead, we’re going to build our understanding **from the ground up**.

We’ll start with:

**What exactly is AI?**

Then move through:

**Machine Learning → Deep Learning → Neural Networks → Transformers → LLMs → Embeddings → RAG → Agents → Evaluation → Production AI → AI System Design**

And eventually:

**How do you actually become an AI engineer?**

**Who is this for?**

This is especially for **women transitioning into AI**.

Maybe you’re a:

- Software Engineer
- Data Analyst
- Business Analyst
- Product Manager
- Researcher
- Graduate student
- Or someone with a technical background who’s trying to figure out where AI fits into your career.

You don’t need to know everything before starting.

You don’t need a PhD.

You don’t need to understand every new framework that gets released.

And you definitely don’t need to pretend that you understand something when you don’t.

**We’ll start with the basics and build from there.**

**What we’re going to cover**

**🧠 Part 1 — Understanding AI**

**Week 1:** AI vs ML vs Deep Learning vs Generative AI  
**Week 2:** How Machine Learning Actually Works  
**Week 3:** Neural Networks & Deep Learning  
**Week 4:** Transformers & Attention

**🤖 Part 2 — Understanding LLMs**

**Week 5:** How LLMs Actually Work  
**Week 6:** Prompts, Tokens, Context & Model Behavior  
**Week 7:** RAG — From Documents to Answers  
**Week 8:** Advanced RAG & Retrieval

**🏗️ Part 3 — Building AI Systems**

**Week 9:** AI Agents  
**Week 10:** Agentic & Multi-Agent Systems  
**Week 11:** AI Evaluation & Reliability  
**Week 12:** Production AI

**🚀 Part 4 — Becoming AI-Ready**

**Week 13:** AI System Design  
**Week 14:** AI Engineering Skills  
**Week 15:** Transitioning Into an AI Role  
**Week 16:** Putting It All Together

And this won’t just be theory.

We’ll look at **code, architectures, real-world examples, tradeoffs, projects and interview concepts** along the way.

**But there’s another reason I’m doing this.**

I don’t believe confidence comes from knowing everything.

**Confidence comes from having evidence that you can figure things out.**

You understand embeddings → evidence.

You build a RAG system → evidence.

You debug a retrieval problem → evidence.

You finally understand attention → evidence.

You solve a coding problem you couldn’t solve two weeks ago → evidence.

Slowly, you stop thinking:

*“Maybe I’m not smart enough for AI.”*

And start thinking:

**“I don’t know this yet. But I can learn it.”**

That distinction matters.

**And eventually, this will become something bigger.**

I’m going to document everything here.

The explanations.

The diagrams.

The examples.

The code.

The things that confused me.

The things that finally clicked.

And eventually, I want to turn the entire journey into an **open AI learning resource on GitHub** — and, someday, into a book for people making the transition into AI.

So this isn’t just 16 weeks of posts.

It’s the beginning of a **living AI curriculum.**

**One concept at a time.**

If you’ve been telling yourself:

*“I’ll start learning AI when I have more time.”*

Maybe this is your sign.

You don’t have to learn everything today.

You don’t even have to understand everything this week.

**Just start with one concept.**

I’ll do the same.

**16 weeks. One direction. One concept at a time.**

Let’s see how far we can go. 🚀
